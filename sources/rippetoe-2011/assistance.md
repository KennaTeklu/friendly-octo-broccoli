# Starting Strength — Assistance / Accessory Lifts

Source: Mark Rippetoe, Starting Strength: Basic Barbell Training, 3rd edition (2011).
Chapter: Programming — Assistance Exercises.

Rippetoe prescribes assistance lifts as 3 sets of 8-12 reps at moderate intensity,
after the main compound lift. The purpose is to add volume to muscles that the
main lift under-trains.

## Table — Assistance Lift Prescription

| Lift     | Sets | Reps | %1RM    | Rest   | Purpose |
|----------|------|------|---------|--------|---------|
| RDL      | 3    | 8-12 | 60-70   | 2-3 min | Posterior chain volume |
| Chin-Up  | 3    | 5-8  | BW      | 2 min   | Lat/biceps volume |
| Dips     | 3    | 6-10  | BW      | 2 min   | Chest/triceps volume |
| Back Ext | 3    | 10-15| BW      | 1-2 min | Erector endurance |

## Notes

- Assistance lifts are performed AFTER the main lift, not instead of it.
- Progress on assistance is slower — add 5 lbs every 2-3 sessions, not every session.
- RDL uses 60-70% of deadlift 1RM as the reference, not a separate 1RM test.
- Chin-ups and dips use bodyweight. When the trainee can do 3×8 chin-ups,
  add weight (weighted chin-up) using a belt.
- No 1RM test needed for assistance lifts. Use the main lift's 1RM as the reference.
