# Gymnastic Body — Leverage Progression Tiers

Source: Christopher Sommer, Building the Gymnastic Body (2006).
Chapter: Pull-Up Progressions.

The progression replaces load with leverage. No weight is added.
Difficulty increases by changing body position or lever arm.

## Table — Pull-Up Progression

| Tier | Name           | Difficulty | Sets | Reps   | Rest |
|------|---------------|------------|------|--------|------|
| 1    | Band-assisted  | 1/10       | 3    | 5-8    | 90s  |
| 2    | Negative only  | 2/10       | 3    | 3-5    | 90s  |
| 3    | Jump-assisted  | 3/10       | 3    | 3-5    | 90s  |
| 4    | Chin-up         | 4/10       | 3    | 3-8    | 120s |
| 5    | Pull-up        | 5/10       | 3    | 3-8    | 120s |
| 6    | Wide pull-up   | 6/10       | 3    | 3-6    | 120s |
| 7    | L-sit pull-up  | 7/10       | 3    | 3-5    | 120s |
| 8    | Archer pull-up | 8/10       | 3    | 2-4    | 150s |
| 9    | Typewriter     | 9/10       | 3    | 2-3    | 150s |
| 10   | One-arm pull-up| 10/10      | 3    | 1-3    | 180s |

## Progression rule

Advance to the next tier when the trainee completes the top of the rep
range for all sets. Do not add weight — increase leverage difficulty.

## Notes

- Tier replaces weight entirely. No 1RM. No load field.
- Rest increases with tier because neurological demand increases.
- This table covers pull-ups. Push-up and dip progressions follow
  the same leverage model but with different tier names.
