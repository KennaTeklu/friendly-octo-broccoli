# 23 - Forgetting

We designed the system to remember everything forever. That is wrong.

## Why forgetting matters

Some things must be released. A coach who remembers every mistake holds a grudge the user has already moved past. A coach who remembers every injury forever prescribes around a shoulder that healed three years ago.

Forgetting is a feature. Not a bug.

## What to forget

- Old injuries that have cleared. After 12 months with no recurrence, an injury is not active context.
- Past plateaus. If the user stalled on bench for 6 weeks two years ago and has been progressing since, the plateau is history, not a fact.
- Bad decisions. The user did not deload when they should have. It was 18 months ago. It is not the current self.
- Old PRs that no longer represent capacity. A peak from a year ago is not a baseline for today.
- Specific sessions. Individual sessions fade into weekly summaries after 12 weeks, monthly summaries after a year.
- Specific RPE reports. Only the trend matters after 6 months.

## The forgetting schedule

| Data | Full retention | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Body signals | 4 weeks | Aggregate to weekly |
| Sessions | 12 weeks | Aggregate to weekly |
| Weekly summaries | 12 months | Aggregate to monthly |
| Monthly summaries | 5 years | Aggregate to seasonal |
| Injuries cleared | 12 months after clear | Mark as historical |
| PRs from peak | 12 months | Mark as reference peak |
| Tuning intents | Active only | Archive when changed |
| Notes | 12 months | Summarize |

## What the coach never forgets

- The user's philosophy
- The user's permission model
- Current phase and arc
- Active restrictions and injuries
- The chronicle narrative
- The user's story as they tell it

## The principle

Remember the pattern. Forget the particulars.

## The user's control

The user can:

- Mark anything as "forget this"
- Mark anything as "remember forever"
- Export the full log before forgetting
- Never lose their own data

The app forgets. The user keeps whatever they want.
